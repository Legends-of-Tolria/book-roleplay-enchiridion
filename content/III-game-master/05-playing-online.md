# Playing Online

## Questions to ask yourself

1. Do your players want a full rules engine, or just a map and some tokens?
2. Will everyone play in a browser, or can they install something?
3. Who pays, and is it once or every month?
4. How much prep time do you have before the first session?

## Technique: start smaller than you think

A virtual tabletop can automate everything, and learning it can eat your prep time. For a first online game, a shared map, tokens and a voice call are enough. Add automation once the group knows what slows them down.

## Virtual tabletops

| Tool | What it's for | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Owlbear Rodeo](https://www.owlbear.rodeo/) | A light, fast browser tabletop: maps, tokens, fog, dice, with extensions | Free to start; paid tiers |
| [Roll20](https://roll20.net/) | Browser tabletop with character sheets and support for many systems | Free to start; paid tiers |
| [Foundry VTT](https://foundryvtt.com/) | Deep, extensible tabletop you host yourself, with a large module community | One-time licence for the game master |
| [Fantasy Grounds](https://www.fantasygrounds.com/) | Desktop tabletop with heavy rules automation and official licensed content | Paid |
| [TaleSpire](https://www.talespire.com/) | A 3D tabletop where you build the scene like a diorama | Paid |
| [Alchemy](https://www.alchemyrpg.com/) | A cinematic tabletop focused on atmosphere, music and scenes | See site |
