# Licences in Plain Words

## Questions to ask yourself

1. Is everything in your project yours: words, art, maps, fonts?
2. If you use someone else's game rules, what licence are they under?
3. Do you want others to be able to build on your work?
4. Will you sell it?

## The short version

*This is a plain-words guide, not legal advice.*

- **Copyright** is automatic. You own what you make. Others need your permission to copy it.
- **Public domain** means no one owns it any more. Use it freely.
- **Creative Commons** licences give permission in advance. **CC0** asks nothing, **CC BY** asks for credit, **BY-SA** asks you to share alike, **NC** means non-commercial only.
- **Game licences** let you publish material for someone else's game. The D&D System Reference Document is under CC BY 4.0, and the ORC License covers Pathfinder and many others.
- **Stock art** comes with its own licence. Read it: some forbid using art in a logo or reselling it on its own.
- **Fonts** have licences too. Most on Google Fonts are free for commercial use.

## Technique: keep a credits file

Every time you use something you didn't make, note what it is, where it came from, the licence and the credit line. Your future self will thank you.
