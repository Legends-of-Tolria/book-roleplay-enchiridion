# Stock Art and Public Domain Sources

## Questions to ask yourself

1. Will this image appear in something you sell, or only in something you share for free?
2. Do you need to change it, or use it as it is?
3. Can you credit the source where the image appears?
4. Does it need to match the look of the rest of your work?

## Technique: read the licence first

Every image comes with four answers, usually on a page called *Licence* or *Terms*: can I use it commercially, do I have to credit anyone, can I change it, and can I resell it as it is? Write those four answers next to the file when you download it. Six months later you will not remember where it came from.

Public domain art from museums is the safest ground. The work is old enough that nobody owns it any more, and the museum says so in writing.

Two cautions. Free stock sites let you use an image but almost never let you sell it unchanged, as a print or a stock image of your own. And look closely at what you download: if your project promises hand-made art, check that the image actually is.

## Museums and public domain collections

| Source | What you'll find | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [The Met Open Access](https://www.metmuseum.org/hubs/open-access) | Over 490,000 images of public domain artworks: paintings, arms and armour, maps, manuscripts | CC0, any use |
| [Smithsonian Open Access](https://www.si.edu/openaccess) | Millions of 2D and 3D items across the Smithsonian's museums and archives | Free reuse without asking |
| [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/free-images-and-open-access) | More than 60,000 downloadable images from its collection | Free for any use, including commercial |
| [Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection) | Dutch masters, prints, ship models, natural history drawings | Check each object's rights line |
| [Old Book Illustrations](https://www.oldbookillustrations.com/) | Engravings and illustrations scanned from old books, searchable by artist and subject | Check each image's page |
| [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/) | Tens of millions of files, many public domain | Varies by file; read the licence box |

## Free stock libraries

| Source | What you'll find | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/) | Photographs: landscapes, textures, people | Free, credit appreciated; can't be sold without significant changes ([licence](https://unsplash.com/license)) |
| [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/) | Photographs and video | Free; read the licence for limits |
| [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/) | Photos, illustrations, video and audio | Free, no credit needed; can't be sold unchanged ([licence](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/)) |
| [Openverse](https://openverse.org/) | A search engine across openly licensed images and audio | Each result shows its own licence |

## Stock art made for games

Paid, but drawn for exactly this: fantasy and science fiction art sold with a licence to use it in your own published games and books. Each pack spells out its terms, usually including commercial use with credit.

| Source | What you'll find | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [DriveThruRPG](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/) | The largest storefront for tabletop games, with a big stock art section alongside adventures and rulebooks | Paid packs; prices vary |
| [Dean Spencer](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/browse/pub/8135/Dean-Spencer-Art) | Full-page scenes, characters, creatures, maps and fillers in colour and line art from a veteran fantasy illustrator ([his site](https://www.deanspencerart.com/)) | Paid; low per-piece prices |
| [Publisher's Choice](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/browse?keyword=publisher%27s%20choice) | Rick Hershey's long-running Publisher's Choice Quality Stock Art line: characters, monsters and scenes sized for book layouts | Paid |
